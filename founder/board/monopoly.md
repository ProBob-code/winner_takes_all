# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong

**The timing answer is real, and it is not the one the founder is giving.** The
2025 Act did not merely threaten WTA; it removed the entire pooled-stakes sector
from the Indian market on 1 May 2026. That is a vacuum, and "the incumbents were
legislated out and the lawful category has no credentialed operator yet" is a
legitimate answer to the lens's "why now" question. It is just an answer to a
different question than the current product was built to ask.

**The build answers the people question.** A solo founder shipped 68 routes, a
balance-gated single-transaction wallet ledger, webhook-verified top-ups, a live
scoring engine, streaming, an admin payout queue and 119 tests behind a CI gate,
at near-zero running cost. That is execution most funded teams do not reach, and
it is the only thing here arguing for backing the founder.

**There is a small market hiding in the brief.** Not players — hosts. Cafés and
organisers running brackets, weekly series, QR broadcast tokens and arenas.
Hosts are findable and repeat. If a lawful WTA exists, it starts there.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

**1. The moat is "nobody has noticed yet", which is a countdown.** Stakes in,
rake out, prize to the winner is the exact structure the Act bans, skill defence
explicitly removed. Three years and ₹1 crore attach to the founder personally
unless there is a company nobody has mentioned. The binding constraint arrives
before any court does: banks and PSPs are barred from processing for money
games, so Razorpay is a single switch somebody else owns. The site's disclaimer
now documents which defence the founder was relying on.

**2. In the lawful version there is no monopoly and no revenue line.** The
carve-out permits fees that cover the cost of running the competition. Cost
recovery is not a 7% rake. Strip staking out and WTA is tournament-hosting
software with sponsor-funded prizes — a sponsorship business needing
relationships and working capital the brief does not evidence. Worse: the gate
is NSGA recognition plus OGA registration. Licensing moats are won by
institutional relationships, not by 23,000 lines of TypeScript, and no
relationship is named. On the engineering question, an 8-ball and football
scoring engine is incremental, not ten times better than anything.

**3. Distribution is absent at both ends.** Five accounts, word of mouth, ₹28 of
revenue per ₹400 pot. Advertising money games is banned outright, so the current
model's only paid channel is closed by statute, and the lawful model proposes no
channel at all. The network effect is a per-café island: every new venue
restarts acquisition from zero and nothing compounds across them. At ₹28 a
tournament, acquisition cost must stay near zero forever, which is not a plan.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Has any entry fee been collected or any payout made since 1 May 2026? How much
  money sits in the wallet ledger today, and whose is it?
- Is there a company, or does this trade on you personally? Have you taken
  written Indian counsel, from whom?
- Who at the Online Gaming Authority have you spoken to, and is 8-ball pool a
  recognised discipline under NSGA 2025 at all?
- Under cost-recovery-only fees: what do you charge, to whom, and who funds the
  prizes?
- Mark which of the 68 routes survive a no-stakes model. What fraction of the
  asset is wallet, rake, settlement and payout machinery?
- Name the cafés and organisers. How many, what do they pay you, and what do
  they use on nights you are not watching?
- What do you know about café tournament nights that a licensed incumbent with
  lawyers does not?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

Not applicable — the vote is PASS. What would bring it back to the table:
staking switched off and the ledger unwound under counsel's sign-off; a lawful
revenue line that is not the rake; ten paying hosts before any licence
conversation. With those three, this is a different and fundable company.
