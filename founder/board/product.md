# Product lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

**The craft in the parts nobody can see is real.** A wallet where every mutation
is a balance-gated single-transaction SQL update, webhook-verified top-ups,
rotating opaque sessions, 119 tests gating every push. A builder who cuts corners
where customers cannot look will cut them where they can. This one does not.
From one person, it is the most fundable signal here.

**The pitch is one sentence, and the name is the pitch.** Pay in, play, winner
takes the pot. Most products never get that clear.

**It understands a physical moment.** Live 8-ball scoring, QR broadcast tokens,
arenas for local score-keeping: someone has watched a café pool table and built
for that room.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

**1. The one thing it does brilliantly is the one thing that is now illegal.**
Under this lens the experience *is* the product, and the experience here is a
stake: the pot on screen, the rake, the payout. Not a feature behind a flag —
the spine, with the name welded to it. Since 1 May 2026 that spine has been prohibited,
and the lines that make the experience distinctive are the lines that must go.
Worse, the "outcomes are determined solely by participant skill" disclaimer is a
seam the product already knows about: a legal fiction stitched over the UI. A
product that must deny what it plainly is has not been designed, it has been
argued.

**2. Nothing was ever said no to.** Thirty pages, sixty-eight routes, two sports,
series, rankings, streaming, QR, arenas, wallet, payouts, admin console — for
five accounts. Focus means cutting until one thing is left; here the surface grew
instead. Every page is maintenance the solo founder owes forever, and almost
none of it has met a stranger.

**3. The experience is handed off where the customer cares most.** Razorpay to
wallet to pot to settlement to an admin approval queue to UPI. That queue is one
human being, awake or not, standing between a winner and his money. Banks are
also barred from processing payments for this model, so the rail itself is a seam
the founder does not control. Winning and then waiting is the memory players
repeat to everyone else.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- What is the one thing? Say it without the words "pot," "wallet" or "rake."
- The first five minutes for a player who has never seen it: how many screens
  before the first shot, and how many of them are money?
- If the pot vanished tomorrow, what would make someone open this on a Tuesday
  night? Does anything the five accounts did suggest an answer?
- Where does a host hand off to the platform, and what breaks there?
- Who is the customer, the player or the café owner? Pick one, and say why.
- Which of the thirty pages would you delete this week, and what breaks?
- How many payouts have you approved by hand, and what was the longest wait?
- Of the five accounts: how many tournaments ran start to finish, and how many
  people came back?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Ship a version with no stake and prove people still play.** Entry funded by
   a host, a sponsor or a subscription, never pooled from players. The measure:
   completed tournaments per week with zero rupees staked. If the experience only
   works with money on it, the product was the money.
2. **Pick one customer and cut to one surface.** My read: the organiser, and the
   product is "run a bracket on the café screen in sixty seconds." Then the
   wallet, rake and payout queue leave the player app, deleted not hidden.
3. **Retire the stake UI, the skill-game disclaimer and the name together,
   now — the 1 May 2026 deadline has already passed.** Keeping a name that advertises the banned structure is a
   seam every regulator and customer reads first.
4. **No customer-facing moment may depend on the founder being awake.** Either
   no payouts, or automatic settlement with a published time.
5. **Ten organisers who are not friends run a bracket end to end unassisted,
   before another feature is written.**
