# Offers lens
Score: 3 | Vote: FUND IF

The score is for the offer, not the code. The offer as written is unsellable from
1 May 2026. The FUND IF is for a different offer to a different buyer.

## What is strong

**Speed to outcome.** The lens scores value down for time delay, and this is the
one place the product excels: a buyer pays, plays and is paid the same evening.
Most offers die waiting. This resolves in an hour.

**The dream outcome needs no explaining.** "Pay ₹100, win the pot" needs no
education and no trust in a vague claim.

**The delivery half of a stack exists.** Brackets, live scoring for two games,
series standings, arenas, QR broadcast, streaming, an admin console — these solve
the problems a *host* hits running a tournament night, built and deployed at
near-zero cost. The lens wants things cheap to deliver and valuable to the buyer;
most of this qualifies.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

**1. What the buyer pays for is the illegal part.** The stake *is* the offer.
Strip it and you have not weakened the offer, you have deleted its dream outcome
— nobody pays ₹100 to be drawn into a bracket for nothing. This is not a pivot of
an offer; it is an admission the offer must be replaced and the buyer changed from
player to host. No evidence a host wants to buy software exists: five accounts,
word of mouth, no software revenue. The offer restarts at zero against a sunk
asset.

**2. The market fails the reachability test, by law.** The lens puts "easy to find
and reach" ahead of offer quality. Advertising money games is banned and banks are
barred from processing them. The channel is not expensive, it is closed. The
downside is personal — three years and ₹1 crore — against ₹28 a tournament. No
offer justifies that trade. The site's skill disclaimer asserts a defence the 2025
Act specifically removed; that is not protection, it is a record of intent.

**3. The unit economics cannot carry an offer.** ₹28 gross on a ₹400 pot, less
Razorpay on the top-up and 1% back on withdrawal. The lens says strengthen an
offer with a guarantee, bonuses and urgency, and never compete on price. Here the
only differentiator *is* being a cheaper way to settle a bet — and friends with
cash on the table already pay 0%, instantly, with no wallet and no approval
queue. A commodity toll losing to free. Double the rake and it ends that week.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Of five accounts, how many played twice? Total GMV to date?
- What does Razorpay cost per top-up and payout, so what is the real net on ₹28?
- Is there money in user wallets now, and what returns it?
- Which café or organiser asked for this unprompted? Did any name a price for the
  software alone, with no pot flowing through it?
- What is the host's dream outcome in their words — more covers on a dead
  Tuesday? A league their regulars return for? What proof exists?
- Have you taken Indian counsel on liability for tournaments already run?
- Is NSGA recognition plus Online Gaming Authority registration achievable by one
  unfunded person, at what cost?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. Stop taking stakes now, return wallet balances, delete the skill disclaimer.
   Not conditional on anything else.
2. Remove participant money entirely — no wallet, no rake, no payouts. Prize
   money, if any, comes from the venue or a sponsor, never pooled from entrants.
3. Rebuild the offer for the host, priced as software: a flat monthly fee per
   venue, named, with a guarantee near-zero running costs make affordable — run
   your league night on it, and if your regulars do not come back, you do not pay.
4. Three hosts paying at that price before another feature is written. If three
   cannot be found by hand, the offer is wrong, not the price.
5. Then test at twice the price. If they still say yes, come back.
